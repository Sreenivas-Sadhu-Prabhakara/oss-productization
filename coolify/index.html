Coolify productization — a self-hosted PaaS as your deploy foundation

Coolify is an open-source Heroku / Vercel alternative you run on your own servers. The most useful way to productize it isn't to resell hosting — it's to make it the reproducible, access-gated control plane that everything else deploys onto.

What it is

A PaaS you own, that deploys other software for you.

Coolify installs on a Linux host and then manages your target servers over SSH + Docker. It builds and deploys apps (Git push, Dockerfile, docker-compose, Nixpacks), provisions databases, ships one-click service templates for hundreds of popular tools, handles automatic TLS, scheduled backups, webhooks, and secret/env management. The upstream is primarily a Laravel/PHP + Livewire application.

That feature set is why it's the natural base of a productization program: the other tools in these guides become Coolify services, not bespoke infrastructure. Codify the deploy groove once, reuse it for every tenant.

The license reality

The freest position in the set.

Apache-2.0 — permissive, with a patent grant

You may fork it, rebrand it, build a closed-source wedge on top, and run it as a commercial service. There is no copyleft and no branding clause, and Apache-2.0 adds an explicit patent grant on top of MIT-style freedom. For productization this is the easy case — nothing in the license blocks a business model.

The only real limit is positioning, not law: a "managed Coolify hosting" product competes directly with the vendor's own Coolify Cloud. That's a commercial choice to make deliberately — it isn't a licensing constraint.

How you'd productize it

Make it the foundation, not the product.

Two framings, in order of leverage:

  • M3 · foundation (recommended). Coolify is the control plane other open-source tenants deploy onto. The wedge is three parts: provisioning-as-code (a one-command, reviewed stand-up — never a blind curl | bash), managed ops (backups with a tested restore, monitoring, access-gating), and a branded entry surface (Apache-2.0 permits it freely). Codify a "deploy-a-tenant" runbook once and reuse it.
  • M1 · managed host. Offer Coolify itself as a hosted product with an SLA and support. Legal under Apache-2.0 — just weigh the positioning note above.

The groove

Pin-and-wedge, concretely.

  1. Pin the upstream at a release (here, v4.7.6) and treat it as a tracked dependency, not a float.
  2. Stand up on a Linux host from the official installer — downloaded and reviewed before it runs, not piped straight into a shell.
  3. Harden access: firewall to 22/80/443, put the UI behind a gated domain — never leave the raw admin port open.
  4. Deploy a tenant via a native service template, then wire scheduled backups + a restore you've actually tested before calling it done.

The catch

It holds the keys to everything.

Control plane = blast radius

Coolify stores the SSH keys to every server it manages and the secrets/env of every app it runs, and it can deploy or destroy across all of them. That makes it the highest-value target you'll operate — compromise the control plane and you've compromised the whole fleet.

Non-negotiables: access-gate the UI, use least-privilege SSH, encrypt backups, and keep real tenant workloads on worker servers Coolify manages — not on the control-plane box itself.
