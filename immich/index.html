Immich productization — self-hosted photos as a managed product

Immich is a self-hosted Google Photos: backup, mobile apps, and ML search over your own library. Its license is the whole game — productize it by hosting it unmodified and selling a separate app that talks to its API.

What it is

A private photo cloud with real ML.

Immich backs up photos and video from mobile and web, then runs CLIP-style semantic search and face recognition over them. The stack is a NestJS/TypeScript server, a SvelteKit web app, Flutter mobile apps, PostgreSQL with a vector extension for embeddings, Redis, and a Python ML service. It deploys from an official docker-compose, and self-hosted PaaS platforms ship a one-click template for it.

It is also the highest-sensitivity data in this set: entire personal photo libraries, EXIF geolocation, and biometric face data. Privacy isn't a feature here — it's the product promise.

The license reality

AGPL means the network counts as distribution.

AGPL-3.0 — strong copyleft + the §13 network clause

Hosting Immich unmodified as a service is completely fine. But if you modify it and let users reach it over a network, AGPL's §13 obliges you to offer those users the modified source. Patch Immich to build your feature and you've just agreed to publish your feature.

The way through: don't patch it. Keep your product a separate, non-derivative application that talks to Immich over its public API. Your app stays yours; Immich stays unmodified; the clause is satisfied.

How you'd productize it

Immich is the engine; the wedge is the product.

Run Immich as a managed private photo cloud (M1), and add one workflow that the bare project doesn't have. The sharp example is client-gallery delivery for photographers: a separate service that calls Immich's API to publish branded, expiring, access-controlled galleries to clients.

  • Who pays: photographers and studios who want a private library and a polished client-delivery experience, without a public cloud.
  • Why it's defensible under AGPL: the gallery app is API-only and non-derivative, so you can brand and even close it — Immich underneath stays untouched and upgradeable.

The groove

Pin-and-wedge, concretely.

  1. Pin Immich at a release (here, v3.4.2) — it moves fast and has a pre-1.0 history of breaking changes, so re-test on every bump.
  2. Deploy it unmodified (compose or a native PaaS template); access-gate it behind auth and TLS.
  3. Build the wedge as a separate service against Immich's API — albums, assets, shared links — never a patch to Immich itself.
  4. Back up Postgres and the originals volume, and run a restore into a scratch target to prove assets return.

The catch

Two: the AGPL trap, and the storage economics.

Don't patch upstream

The single most expensive mistake is a "quick fix" inside the Immich source. That turns your whole deployment into a modified network service and triggers the source-offer obligation. Architect the wedge as API-only from day one.

And the quieter one: a managed photo service lives or dies on storage cost. Originals are large, backups double them, and biometric/geolocation data raises real privacy-regulation duties. Price the storage and write a retention policy before you take a paying customer.
